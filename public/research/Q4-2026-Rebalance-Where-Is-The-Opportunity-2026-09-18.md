# Where Is the Opportunity Going Into Q4? A Divergence Screen

*Research note — 18 September 2026. Prepared as the broad framing document for the Q4 rebalance. Pre-tax. Prices as of the 18 September session.*

---

## Summary

The screen that produced Credo and Marvell was not a valuation screen. It was an observation that a physical bottleneck existed and that the companies solving it were not priced as though anyone had noticed. That is the shape worth looking for again.

Running the same question across the book and its neighbours, **the market in September 2026 is paying up for narrative and discounting contracted, physical, policy-independent cash flow.** Three divergences are large enough to act on, and one popular idea fails on arithmetic.

## 1. The cybersecurity idea is right and unbuyable

The thesis is sound — quantum risk to current encryption, and recursive self-improvement raising the offensive-capability ceiling faster than defensive budgets adjust. Cyber spend goes up.

The arithmetic refuses it anyway. **CRWD at 81× FY2031 consensus EPS carries a PEG of 3.6.** That is not "expensive for a good business"; it is a price that requires 22% annual compounding for four years *and then* a premium multiple on the result. PANW sits 52% above its 200-day, FTNT 50% above, both within a few percent of all-time highs.

You were right, and the useful conclusion is: **hold the cyber exposure already owned (FTNT, 3.0%), do not add, and revisit only on a drawdown of 30%+.** A correct thesis at the wrong price is a way to lose money slowly.

## 2. Three divergences worth acting on

### (a) Defense — record backlogs, stocks below trend

LMT holds **$230.4B of backlog, up 19% in six months**, on a quarter where book-to-bill exceeded 3×. GD holds **$136.5B, up 32% year-on-year**, every segment at a record. Both stocks trade below their 200-day. NOC is 32% off its high.

The market has decided conversion is the constraint, not demand — and consensus agrees, modelling 4.1–4.4% revenue growth against backlogs building four times faster. Either estimates rise or the factories genuinely cannot go faster. **LMT at a 6% premium to its own long-run median is the way to take that bet; GD at a 20% premium is not.** *(Full analysis in the companion note.)*

### (b) Independent power — contracts intact, curve softened

CEG −38% and VST −36% from their highs, both below their 200-day, while the PPAs that justified them remain contracted for twenty years to investment-grade counterparties. The decline traces to ERCOT forward curves near $30/MWh, not to anything a data centre did. **VST at 8.7× 2030 estimates with 16.5% earnings growth is the single best risk-adjusted number on the board.** *(Full analysis in the companion note.)*

### (c) AI infrastructure hardware — the Credo setup, again

This is the one that most resembles the optical-interconnect call, and it deserves the most attention.

|---|---:|
| CRDO | **−43.0%** |
| ALAB | **−39.3%** |
| VRT | **−34.4%** |

Over the same period hyperscaler capex guidance went from roughly $410B (2025) to **$720–745B (2026)** — an 80% increase, unrevised, from Amazon (~$220B), Google ($195–205B), Microsoft (~$175B) and Meta ($130–145B).

**Three critical-path hardware suppliers de-rated 34–43% into an 80% increase in the spending that buys their product.** That is the same structural mispricing as 2024's interconnect call: the market is trading these as semiconductor beta rather than as suppliers to a contracted, physically-constrained buildout.

**The bottleneck has moved.** It is no longer chips. It is power delivery and thermal management between the substation and the rack — interconnect queues, transformers, switchgear, busway, liquid cooling. VRT sells exactly that, and it is the only one of the three trading *below* its 200-day. If the Q4 rebalance takes one idea from this note, **Vertiv is the closest thing to the Credo trade available today.**

Adjacent names in the same bottleneck worth diligence before committing: **GEV** (already held in FCI — turbines and grid equipment), **POWL** and **ETN** (electrical distribution equipment).

## 3. What to avoid

- **Solar manufacturing.** Cheap for cause: Chinese oversupply above 80% global share, and a US policy cliff requiring construction started after 4 July 2026 to be in service by end-2027. FSLR screens at 6.4× but a majority of that EPS is the Section 45X credit — a policy bet, not a value stock.
- **ARRY specifically.** 4.7× forward earnings, needs only a 9.3× exit to double, and consensus has EPS *declining* after 2028 on flat revenue with the stock at its 52-week low. The cheapest thing on the screen is the one to avoid.
- **Adding to AMD.** +59% above its 200-day after $154 → $560 in a year. Nothing wrong with the business; everything stretched about the entry.

## 4. The concentration problem nobody has raised

Independent of any single idea: the growth sleeve holds roughly **28% across NVDA, MRVL, TSM, CRDO, AMD, NXPI and KEYS**, plus **6% in VST**, which is the same AI-demand factor wearing a utility costume. That is a third of the sleeve in one bet.

Every divergence identified above argues for *more* exposure to that theme. That is precisely when concentration should be decided deliberately rather than drifted into. **Recommend the committee set an explicit cap on aggregate AI-linked exposure before adding, not after.**

## Method and limits

This is a divergence screen across the existing book and its immediate neighbours — roughly thirty names — not a scan of the full market. Its logic is deliberately narrow: find places where price moved a long way and the fundamental did not, then ask which side is wrong. It will miss opportunities in sectors we do not already touch. If the committee wants genuine breadth for Q4, the next step is a systematic screen across the S&P 1500 on the same two axes, which is a larger piece of work and worth commissioning separately.

All multiples are consensus estimates from FMP as of 18 September 2026 and carry the usual caveat that terminal-year estimates rest on thin analyst coverage. CRWD's FY2031 EPS has four estimates behind it; NVDA's FY2031 range runs $13.46 to $23.65.
