# Record Backlogs, Falling Stocks: Does the Defense Book Justify More Weight in Q4?

*Research note — 18 September 2026. Prepared in response to a proposal to raise weight in LMT and GD into Q4 on the strength of wartime order flow. Pre-tax. Prices as of the 18 September session.*

---

## Summary

Both holdings are carrying the largest backlogs in their history, and both stocks are below their 200-day moving average. That divergence is the whole question.

| Backlog | **$230.4B** | **$136.5B** |

**Recommendation: add to LMT, do not add to GD.** The order flow is real at both, but only one of them is being offered at a sane price relative to its own history, and it is the one with the bigger book.

---

## The order flow is not in doubt

Lockheed closed Q2 2026 with **$230.4 billion** of backlog, up from $193.6 billion at year-end — a 19% build in six months — on **$65 billion of new orders in the quarter against $20.1 billion of revenue**. That is a book-to-bill above 3×. Revenue grew 11% to $20.1 billion and management raised full-year guidance to $79.75–81.75 billion. Missiles and Fire Control, the segment most directly geared to munitions replenishment, grew 19% on PAC-3, THAAD and PrSM.

General Dynamics ended the same quarter at **$136.5 billion**, up 32% year-on-year, with every segment at a record. Book-to-bill was 1.4× companywide — Combat Systems 2.1×, Aerospace 1.5×, Technologies 1.1×. Revenue rose 8.1% to $14.1 billion, EPS 13.4% to $4.24, and operating margin widened 40bp to 10.4%.

Neither of those is an ambiguous picture. The demand signal is as strong as this industry produces.

## What the market is saying instead

The sector has de-rated while the books have filled:

| | Off 52-week high | Versus 200-day |
|---|---:|---:|
| LMT | −22.9% | −4.6% |
| GD | −11.7% | −0.6% |
| NOC | **−31.9%** | **−12.3%** |
| RTX | −14.5% | flat |

Four primes with record demand, all below or at their trend. That is not a market pricing a war premium. It is a market that has decided **backlog is not the constraint — conversion is.**

The consensus numbers say the same thing in a different language. LMT's backlog grew 19% in six months; consensus revenue growth for 2026–2030 is **4.4% a year**. GD's grew 32% in a year; consensus revenue growth is **4.1%**. The Street is modelling backlog conversion at roughly a fifth of the rate the backlog is accumulating.

That gap is the investable question, and it has only two resolutions. Either the analysts are too conservative and estimates rise as deliveries accelerate — or the primes genuinely cannot build faster, because of machinists, castings, solid rocket motors and shipyard capacity, and the backlog is a queue rather than a forecast. The second explanation has been right more often than the first for the last decade. Backlog is a claim on future revenue only to the extent the factory exists.

## Why LMT and not GD

Both are good businesses. The difference is what you pay.

**LMT at 17.5× 2026 earnings sits 6% above its own long-run median of 16.5×.** For the largest backlog in the industry, growing fastest, in the segment most levered to munitions demand, a 6% premium is close to no premium at all. On 2028 estimates it is 15.1×, below its own median.

**GD at 20.8× sits 20% above its median of 17.4×.** That is a full re-rating already paid for. GD's backlog multiple is lower (2.44× revenue against LMT's 2.85×), its EPS growth is slower (6.9% against 7.5%), and a meaningful part of its book-to-bill strength is Gulfstream at 1.5× — business jets, which are the most cyclical thing either company sells and the first to be cancelled in a downturn. Paying a 20% premium to own history for a book with a cyclical component in it is the wrong side of this trade.

Put plainly: the war thesis argues for defense exposure. It does not argue for *this* defense exposure at *this* price. LMT gives you the same thesis with a bigger book at a fifth of the valuation premium.

## What would change the call

- **Estimates start rising.** If 2027 consensus revenue moves above ~$88B for LMT, conversion is beating the model and the backlog is worth more than the Street assumes. That is the signal to add again.
- **Margin holds through the ramp.** LMT's classified and F-35 programmes have produced charges before — Q2 2025 EPS was $1.46 against $7.94 this year, almost entirely a charge effect. A repeat resets the thesis.
- **GD to ~17.5×** — around $297 on 2026 estimates — would make it a buy on the same logic being applied to LMT here.
- **Gulfstream order cancellations** would take out the segment carrying GD's book-to-bill.

## Sizing

Move LMT from **4.0% to 5.0%**, funded from the existing cash position or from a trim elsewhere in the sleeve; hold GD at 3.5%. That takes aerospace and defense from 7.5% to 8.5% of the dividend sleeve without adding to the position that is already paying for good news.

If the committee prefers a single action, adding 100bp to LMT alone captures most of the thesis: it is the larger backlog, the faster build, the cheaper multiple against its own record, and the segment mix closest to the actual demand driver.
