## 02 · Macro & Geopolitical Context

The committee should consider these proposals against the following backdrop, which has changed materially since our Q2 rebalance in April:

- **The Iran War arc** defined the quarter: oil topped $100/bbl on April 13, a ceasefire was announced June 17, fresh strikes were exchanged June 25–28 before talks resumed in Doha, and the Strait of Hormuz is now roughly half-open under a quota system, with Gulf flows near 75% of prewar levels. Crude has fully round-tripped its war premium.
- **Refining margins did not round-trip:** crack spreads remain near $54/barrel against a historical norm of $10–20, held up by structural capacity shortages, the Port Arthur outage, and disrupted product flows. This decoupling is the basis for the VLO exit — harvesting peak-cycle economics that consensus itself expects to mean-revert.
- **Reserve restocking:** our research estimates roughly 500 million barrels of global restocking demand, a durable bid under crude even as the war premium unwinds.
- **The Fed** held rates at the June 17 meeting under new Chairman Kevin Warsh while signaling potential hikes; markets price roughly 45bp of tightening. The higher-for-longer regime pressures rate-sensitive utilities, REITs, and unprofitable growth.
- **A quality de-rating** swept data, software, and services compounders: ServiceNow fell more than 50% from its highs on agentic-AI displacement fears, and SPGI, ADP, SYK, and SSNC all trade at the cheapest forward multiples in their published histories. This repricing supplies most of this quarter's buy candidates.
- **The consumer-lag window is approaching:** our oil-shock research places the earnings damage from February's price spike in Q4 2026–Q1 2027, with delinquencies already at post-2012 highs. Consumer-credit and cyclical-consumer exposure is sized accordingly.
- **The power supercycle accelerated:** PJM is fast-tracking data-center interconnections, Walmart signed its first-ever nuclear PPA (with Constellation), Microsoft purchased 3,200 acres in Cheyenne for data-center development, and the DOE committed $17.5B in loans to jumpstart ten new Westinghouse AP1000 reactors. Electricity scarcity is this quarter's central buy-side thesis.

## 03 · The Software Mispricing Opportunity

The central thesis of this rebalance is sector-level: quality software and data businesses with high AI-resilience scores have been repriced as though agentic AI ends their business models. I believe strongly that this is a mispricing, that a recovery is coming, and that it is the quarter's clearest opportunity. IGV (software ETF) sits in a consolidation-before-a-move while hardware trades at all-time highs; underneath, the franchises we are buying or elevating trade at the cheapest forward multiples in their published histories — ServiceNow at ~26x (vs. a 40x–96x history), S&P Global at 21.2x (vs. a 30.3x average), ADP at 21.3x (vs. 27.6x), and SS&C at 9.3x (vs. 13.2x). Each scores 8+ on our AI Resilience factor: the screener's judgment is that proprietary workflows and authoritative data become *more* valuable in an AI world, not less. Several entries require essentially zero earnings growth to break even, and two (ADP, SS&C) carry positive bear cases in our scenario work.

The proposal expresses the view as a sector weight: dedicated Software exposure rises from 8.0% to 10.0% of the growth sleeve and from 5.0% to 12.0% of the dividend sleeve — roughly +2.7% of the total book.

## 04 · Valuation Discipline: Trimming Extended Winners

The mirror image of the software opportunity is valuation discipline among our winners: the same framework that says durable growth is cheap in software says it is expensive in several champions. In each case the business is not in question — the price is. The trims fund the software and financials reweighting, where the same quality trades at record-low multiples.

- **AMD — trim.** At 73x forward earnings it is priced at more than three times NVDA's 22x, for less forward earnings growth (+59% vs. +62% consensus); its forward PEG is 1.24 against NVDA's 0.49.
- **CAT — trim.** The book's highest composite (88) now trades at 40x forward with a 2.29 forward PEG for ~11% growth, after a +73% YTD re-rating.
- **LRCX — capped.** Despite its 78 composite — 69x forward and a 3.11 forward PEG after a +129% YTD run make it the most expensive growth in the dividend sleeve.

## 05 · ISM Manufacturing Update

The ISM Manufacturing PMI registered 53.3 in June — a sixth consecutive month of expansion, the longest streak since 2022 (January 52.6, February 52.4, March 52.7, April 52.7, May 54.0 — a four-year high — June 53.3). New Orders held at 56.0 and Employment improved to 49.7, its best reading since January 2025. The expansion thesis from our April research remains intact: when ISM sustains above 50 for 3+ months after prolonged contraction, cyclical equities have historically outperformed the S&P 500 by 800–1,200 basis points over the subsequent 12 months, with Industrials ranking first.

**Prices Paid — monitor item resolved.** The Q2 trigger (Prices Paid above 80 for two consecutive months) fired in April (84.6) and May (82.1). June then delivered the largest single-month decline since July 2022, dropping 9.1 points to 73.0. The Q2 playbook called for rotating weight from pure cyclicals toward quality industrials if the trigger hit; this proposal executes the first half of that rotation — STLD is trimmed from 7.0% to 4.0% and NTR from 7.0% to 4.5% — but routes the proceeds to the software and financials reweighting rather than to CAT and FAST, whose multiples (40x and 38x forward) now fully price the quality premium. Prices Paid remains elevated versus pre-war levels and stays on the monitor list.
