
# Picks & Shovels: The LRCX and CAT of the Next Era

## The Thesis

The fortunes in a gold rush go to the people selling picks and shovels, not the prospectors. LRCX doesn't care whether TSMC or Samsung wins the foundry war — it sells the etch and deposition tools to both. CAT doesn't care which hyperscaler builds the biggest data center — it sells the gensets and turbines to all of them. These are **toll-booth businesses**: they get paid per unit of industry activity, regardless of which end-player wins.

This report hunts for the next generation of these enablers across the frontier industries — peptides, humanoid robotics, space, quantum computing, AI data-center buildout, advanced semiconductors, and nuclear — plus the rare compounders that sell into *all* of them at once. The screen deliberately avoids the hyped pure-plays (the RGTIs, ASTSs, and single-drug bets) in favor of less-glamorous component, tooling, and capacity suppliers with durable moats.

## What Makes a Great Picks-and-Shovels Play — the Six Tests

Every name below was screened against these:

1. **Toll-booth economics** — paid per unit of industry activity (per wafer, per megawatt, per injectable, per robot), not on one winner's success.
2. **Spec'd-in switching costs** — the product is designed into a regulated filing or a qualified manufacturing process, so changing suppliers means a costly re-qualification or refiling. Near-zero churn.
3. **Razor-blade recurring revenue** — aftermarket parts, consumables, or installed-base service annuities that recur for the life of the equipment.
4. **Competitor-agnostic** — sells to every end-player in the race, not betting on one.
5. **Real, profitable core today** — the frontier theme is *optionality layered on top* of an already-profitable business. This is the single most important filter: it separates an arms-dealer from a speculation.
6. **Oligopoly or monopoly structure** — few credible suppliers, high barriers to entry.

The best names hit all six. The ones to avoid hit only #1 and #4 — the right exposure, but with no moat and no profits (the speculative pure-plays).

## By-Theme Deep Dives

### 1. Peptides / GLP-1 — the toll-booths of the obesity-drug boom

The drug-winner fight (Lilly vs. Novo vs. the orals) is crowded and binary. The durable money is in the components and capacity that sit in *every* GLP-1 supply chain.

- **West Pharmaceutical (WST)** — Conviction 5. The LRCX-of-injectables. Makes the elastomer stoppers, plungers, and seals that go into essentially every injectable GLP-1. The stopper is part of the FDA-approved container system, so switching means a refiling — near-zero churn. GLP-1 components ~9% of revenue and growing double-digits. *Flag: rich (~40x P/E); buy on weakness.*
- **Bachem (BANB, Swiss)** — Conviction 5. The purest large-scale peptide CDMO. Once a sponsor validates a peptide process and files it, re-qualifying a new manufacturer costs years. Guiding 35–45% local-currency growth for 2026 with a >CHF 1B multi-year order book. *Flag: single-facility concentration; rich.*
- **Lonza (LONN, Swiss)** — Conviction 5. The blue-chip diversified CDMO — peptide API *and* fill-finish under one roof. Higher quality precisely because it doesn't live or die on one drug class.
- **Stevanato (STVN)** — Conviction 4. Vials, cartridges, and Nexa pre-fillable syringes; GLP-1 was ~20% of revenue and grew >50%. Cleaner GLP-1 growth beta than West, more reasonably valued.
- **Sartorius Stedim (DIM, Paris)** — Conviction 4. Single-use bioprocessing leader; ~75% recurring consumables. The most reasonably valued large-cap tools name after the post-COVID destock.
- **Danaher (DHR)** — see Tier 1. The quality compounder; bioprocessing orders just turned +30% YoY after a two-year destock.
- *Avoid: Gerresheimer (accounting probe, guidance cuts); Repligen (great franchise, ~50-58x EV/EBITDA — wrong price); Bio-Techne (being acquired by Merck KGaA — arb, not a hold).*

### 2. Humanoid Robotics — own the joints, not the robot

~60–70% of a humanoid's bill of materials is actuators (motors + reducers + screws + bearings) and sensors, dominated by a handful of precision-engineering firms.

- **Harmonic Drive Systems (6324, Tokyo)** — Conviction 5. The LRCX of robot joints. Strain-wave reducers are the standard in precision wrist/hand joints; wide-moat, real revenue today from industrial robots. *Flag: rich, cyclical.*
- **Nabtesco (6268, Tokyo)** — Conviction 4. The other half of the reducer duopoly — cycloidal (RV) reducers for the larger hip/shoulder/leg joints. More diversified, cheaper.
- **THK (6481, Tokyo)** — Conviction 4. Ballscrews and linear guides — the linear "muscle" of humanoid limbs. Optimus Gen-3 uses precision ballscrews in THK's wheelhouse.
- **Amphenol (APH)** / **TE Connectivity (TEL, owned)** — the interconnect oligopoly; content rises with robot complexity.
- **RBC Bearings (RBC)** — Conviction 4. The cleanest US-listed precision-bearings play; aerospace franchise today, humanoid optionality on top. *Flag: ~30x.*
- **Infineon (IFNNY ADR)** — Conviction 4. ~$500 of power-semi content per humanoid; best risk/reward in the chip layer.
- **Allegro Microsystems (ALGM)** — Conviction 3-4. Magnetic position/current sensors, ~$150 content per humanoid; earliest-stage revenue of the group.
- *Note: the purest reducer/linear-motion plays are Japan-listed. Rare-earth magnet names (MP Materials) are policy-driven thematics, not toll-booths — satellite/hedge only.*

### 3. Space — the arms dealers, not the rockets

The launch/satellite pure-plays (RKLB, ASTS, RDW, LUNR) are binary bets on execution and dilution. The content suppliers earn fee-per-vehicle economics on top of a much larger, profitable defense/aerospace base.

- **TransDigm (TDG)** — see Tier 1. The purest toll-booth in aerospace.
- **Howmet (HWM)** — Conviction 5. Investment-cast superalloy turbine blades and structural titanium spec'd into every engine that flies or launches. Captures the value-add (casting/finishing), which is why it beats raw-metal plays like ATI/Carpenter.
- **Amphenol (APH)** — see Tier 1. Content on every satellite, launcher, and missile.
- **Heico (HEI)** — Conviction 4. FAA-PMA aftermarket parts + rad-hard space electronics. Best-in-class but *~62x P/E — stretched; accumulate on pullbacks only.*
- **Microchip (MCHP)** — Conviction 4. Rad-hard/RF silicon designed in for the life of the program. Trade it as a cycle, not a sleep-at-night name.
- **Curtiss-Wright (CW)** — Conviction 4 on quality, but ~33x EV/EBITDA says wait.
- *Avoid: Karman (~198x P/E, hype-adjacent); Comtech (distressed); Viasat (levered operator, too binary).*

### 4. Quantum Computing — own the arms dealers, get quantum for free

The cleanest way to own quantum without the lottery tickets (RGTI, IonQ, Quantinuum) is the firms selling cryogenics, lasers, control electronics, and test gear into *every* qubit modality — each with a large profitable non-quantum core. **Critically, this whole basket is priced off the AI capex cycle, not quantum; the quantum optionality is genuinely free, but the AI multiple is not.**

- **Keysight (KEYS, owned)** — Conviction 5. The cleanest pure toll-booth — you can't build, calibrate, or scale any quantum computer without test & measurement. Just deployed a 1,000-qubit control system with Japan's AIST. Core is RF/microwave/6G/defense test at ~68% gross margin.
- **Analog Devices (ADI, owned)** — Conviction 5. Precision control/RF electronics foundational to the qubit control plane; one of the great analog franchises with quantum as a free kicker.
- **Linde (LIN)** — see Tier 1. With Oxford Instruments having sold its dilution-fridge unit to a private buyer, Linde (helium supply + Bluefors cryo JV) is now the public way to own the cryogenics bottleneck.
- **Coherent (COHR)** — Conviction 4. Photonics breadth feeding ion/atom/photonic systems; the real driver is AI datacenter optics.
- **FormFactor (FORM)** — Conviction 4. Cryogenic probe/test for qubit wafers on top of a semi-test core. *Flag: Street targets sit below market price.*

> **Two of the best quantum toll-booths — KEYS and ADI — are already in the book.** That's strong validation that Family Capital is already executing this philosophy.

- **Eaton (ETN)** — see Tier 1. The #1 pick: "grid-to-chip" electrical distribution, data-center orders +240% YoY, record ~$19.6B backlog, and — unusually for this group — a *reasonable* ~28x valuation, below its own history.
- **Cummins (CMI)** — Conviction 4. High-horsepower gensets for backup power; backlog into 2028. The cheapest name in the group (~23x), though truck-cycle profits dilute the data-center story.
- **GE Vernova (GEV)** — Conviction 4. Gas turbines with slot-reservation agreements through 2030 (the strongest visibility mechanism in the group) + grid + an SMR design. *Flag: ~52x — expensive; size on pullbacks.*
- **nVent (NVT)** — Conviction 4. Spans electrical distribution *and* liquid cooling; better relative value than Vertiv for similar cooling exposure.
- **Quanta Services (PWR)** — Conviction 4. Grid construction where skilled labor is the moat. *Flag: ~50x — richly valued.*
- *Vertiv (VRT): purest power+cooling proxy but ~50x and highest-beta — owns the theme, first to de-rate on any capex scare.*

### 6. Advanced Semiconductors / Packaging / Test — the LRCX-adjacent toll-booths

Family Capital already owns LRCX. The other arms-dealers sell tools/materials to every chipmaker, with the single best structural tailwind being advanced packaging (HBM, CoWoS, hybrid bonding).

- **ASML** & **KLA** — see Tier 1. The monopoly/oligopoly fortresses.
- **Entegris (ENTG)** — Conviction 4. The razor-blade play: CMP slurries, filtration, and specialty gases consumed on every wafer, every cycle — the most recurring model in semicap and far less cyclical than the toolmakers.
- **Besi (BESIY ADR)** — Conviction 4. >90% share in die-to-wafer hybrid bonding — the purest AI-packaging bet. *Flag: ~50x+ — priced for perfection.*
- **Advantest (ADTTF) + Teradyne (TER)** — Conviction 4. The ATE test duopoly; HBM and AI compute require dramatically more test intensity per die. Teradyne offers better entry after a post-earnings pullback.
- **Onto Innovation (ONTO)** — Conviction 4. Advanced-packaging metrology with more diversification than the pure-play Camtek (CAMT, ~50x).

### 7. Nuclear / SMR — own the fuel and the forgings, not the reactor design

SMR startups (Oklo, NuScale, Nano Nuclear) are binary bets on unproven designs. Own the names that win regardless of which reactor prevails.

- **BWX Technologies (BWXT)** — see Tier 1. Monopoly forger of naval and commercial reactor components; sells to all designs. *Flag: ~47x — rich.*
- **Centrus Energy (LEU)** — Conviction 4. The only US-owned enricher and the domestic HALEU chokepoint every advanced reactor needs. $3.9B backlog through 2040. Pairs naturally with Family Capital's CCJ (mining) for full fuel-cycle exposure. *Flag: volatile, policy-driven; size accordingly.*
