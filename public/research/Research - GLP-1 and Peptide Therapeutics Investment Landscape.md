## 2. The Duopoly — Eli Lilly (LLY) vs. Novo Nordisk (NVO)

This is a tale of two companies heading in opposite directions. Lilly is executing a masterclass in pipeline sequencing and manufacturing buildout. Novo is margin-squeezed, losing US market share, and guiding for revenue declines in 2026.

## 3. The Pipeline Beyond LLY and NVO

### Tier 1 — Serious Contenders

#### Amgen (AMG) — MariTide
- **Mechanism:** First-in-class bispecific — GLP-1 agonist + GIP antagonist (vs. Lilly's GIP agonist approach)
- **Dosing:** Monthly injection (vs. weekly for Mounjaro/Zepbound) — massive convenience advantage
- **Phase 2 Data:** ~20% mean weight loss at 52 weeks with no plateau
- **Phase 3 Program:** MARITIME-1 (obesity), MARITIME-2 (obesity + T2D), MARITIME-CV (cardiovascular), MARITIME-HF (heart failure). All four trials running.
- **Readout Timeline:** MARITIME-1 and -2 results expected early 2027
- **Key Risk:** Phase 2 showed high discontinuation and vomiting rates; Amgen adjusted Phase 3 dosing to 3-step escalation (21mg → 35mg → 70mg) to mitigate
- **Investment Angle:** Monthly dosing could differentiate enough to capture meaningful share. AMG is a large-cap with diversified revenue — lower risk way to play the obesity space beyond the duopoly.

#### Viking Therapeutics (VKTX) — VK2735
- **Mechanism:** Dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist (similar to tirzepatide)
- **Phase 2 Data (VENTURE):** Up to 14.7% weight loss after just 13 weekly doses
- **Phase 3 Status:** VANQUISH-1 and VANQUISH-2 fully enrolled (subcutaneous)
- **Oral Program:** Phase 3 initiation for oral VK2735 expected Q4 2026
- **Investment Angle:** Pure-play obesity biotech. High risk/high reward. Potential acquisition target. No revenue — entirely pipeline-driven.

#### Roche (RHHBY) — Carmot Portfolio
- **Acquisition:** Bought Carmot Therapeutics for $2.7B, gaining CT-388, CT-996, CT-868
- **CT-996 (Oral GLP-1):** Phase 1 showed -6.1% weight loss in just 4 weeks. But 85% nausea rate and GERD concerns. Phase 2 readouts expected 2026.
- **CT-388 (Injectable dual GLP-1/GIP):** Weekly injection for obesity ± T2D. Phase 2 data coming.
- **Petrelintide partnership with Zealand Pharma** (see below) — advancing amylin analog to Phase 3
- **Investment Angle:** Roche has stated ambition to be "top three" in obesity. Diversified pipeline approach (oral + injectable + amylin). Large-cap safety.

#### Structure Therapeutics (GPCR) — Aleniglipron
- **Mechanism:** Once-daily oral small molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist
- **Phase 2 Data (ACCESS Program):** 16.3% weight loss at 180mg dose, 16.0% at 240mg dose over 44 weeks. No weight loss plateau.
- **Phase 3 Timeline:** Starting Q3 2026
- **Investment Angle:** One of the most promising oral GLP-1 programs. Small molecule (cheaper to manufacture than peptides). But small-cap biotech risk. Could be an acquisition target for any big pharma wanting an oral entry.

### Tier 2 — Meaningful Pipeline Assets

#### Zealand Pharma (ZEAL) — Petrelintide + Survodutide
- **Petrelintide (Amylin Analog):** Phase 2 ZUPREME-1 showed 10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks with placebo-like tolerability — virtually no nausea or vomiting. Partnered with Roche for Phase 3 (starting H2 2026).
- **Survodutide (Glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonist):** Partnered with Boehringer Ingelheim. Phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-1 showed 16.6% weight loss at 76 weeks. SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD data for liver disease coming at ADA 2026.
- **Investment Angle:** Two differentiated late-stage assets. Petrelintide's tolerability profile is a potential differentiator in a market where GI side effects drive discontinuation. Partnership model de-risks.

#### Altimmune (ALT) — Pemvidutide
- **Mechanism:** GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist
- **Key Milestone:** FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation for MASH (Jan 2026). Phase 3 MASH trial initiation planned for 2026.
- **Phase 2 IMPACT Data:** 48-week data showed significant improvements in fibrosis and inflammation markers
- **Obesity (MOMENTUM):** Phase 2 data presented at ADA 2024
- **Alcohol Use Disorder:** Phase 2 RECLAIM trial ongoing, topline data expected Q3 2026
- **Investment Angle:** MASH + obesity + addiction pipeline. Breakthrough designation in MASH is significant. Micro-cap, high volatility.

#### AstraZeneca (AZN) — Building Via Acquisition
- **Eccogene deal:** Acquired ECC5004 (oral, once-daily) for $185M upfront + up to $1.8B in milestones. Phase 3 initiation planned January 2026.
- **CSPC Collaboration:** Eight next-gen obesity/diabetes programs
- **Investment Angle:** Late entrant but deep pockets. AZN is using its China partnership model to accelerate. No near-term obesity revenue.

### Tier 3 — Earlier Stage / Discontinued

#### Pfizer (PFE) — Danuglipron (DISCONTINUED)
- **Status:** Discontinued April 2025 after liver injury signal in Phase 2
- Pfizer pivoting to oral GIPR antagonist and earlier obesity programs. Effectively out of the near-term race.

#### BioAge Labs — BGE-102
- **Mechanism:** NLRP3-mediated immune response blocker (oral). Designed to combine with GLP-1s.
- **Status:** Phase 1 initiated, topline data expected 2026. Very early stage.

#### Terns Pharmaceuticals (TERN) — Acquired by Merck
- Merck acquired Terns for $6.7B ($53/share) in March 2026 — but this was for TERN-701 (CML oncology program), NOT for obesity.

### Chinese Competitors

#### Sciwind Biotech — Ecnoglutide
- **Mechanism:** First approved cAMP-biased GLP-1 receptor agonist
- **China Approval:** NMPA approved for chronic weight management (March 2026) and T2D (January 2026)
- **Phase Data:** 15.4% weight loss at 48 weeks (2.4mg dose). Head-to-head data shows 35% greater weight loss than semaglutide at Week 20.
- **Pfizer Partnership:** Pfizer China secured exclusive commercialization rights; Sciwind eligible for up to $495M
- **Investment Angle:** China-only for now. Pfizer partnership could be a path to global development.

#### Innovent Biologics — Mazdutide
- **Mechanism:** GCG/GLP-1 dual agonist
- **Status:** ADA 2026 data presentations including three clinical oral presentations. Next-gen oral GLP-1 candidates (IBI3032, IBI3042) in preclinical.
- **IBI3046 combo data:** 20% body weight loss and 80% fat mass reduction in combination studies.
- **Investment Angle:** Leading Chinese obesity player. Could disrupt pricing in Asia-Pacific markets.

## 4. Next-Gen Mechanisms Beyond Basic GLP-1

### The Mechanism Hierarchy (ranked by weight loss efficacy)

| Mechanism | Lead Drug | Phase | Weight Loss | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon Triple Agonist** | Retatrutide (Lilly) | Phase 3 | **28-30%** at 80 weeks | Highest efficacy ever seen; approaches bariatric surgery |
| **GLP-1/Amylin Dual (injectable)** | CagriSema (Novo) | NDA submitted | **22.7%** at 68 weeks | Dual complementary satiety signals |
| **GLP-1/GIP Dual (injectable)** | Tirzepatide (Lilly) | Approved | **20-22%** at 72 weeks | Current gold standard (Mounjaro/Zepbound) |
| **GLP-1 + GIP Antagonist (monthly)** | MariTide (Amgen) | Phase 3 | **~20%** at 52 weeks, no plateau | Monthly dosing — convenience play |
| **Glucagon/GLP-1 Dual** | Survodutide (Zealand/BI) | Phase 3 | **16.6%** at 76 weeks | Liver benefits (MASH potential) |
| **Oral Small Molecule GLP-1** | Aleniglipron (GPCR) | Phase 2 | **16.3%** at 44 weeks | Oral, small molecule, no plateau |
| **GLP-1 Oral Peptide** | Foundayo (Lilly) | Approved | **12.8-14.4%** at 72 weeks | Approved, no food/water restrictions |
| **Amylin Analog (standalone)** | Petrelintide (Zealand/Roche) | Phase 2 | **10.7%** at 42 weeks | Near-zero GI side effects |

### Retatrutide — The Pipeline King

Lilly's retatrutide is the most important drug in the entire obesity pipeline.

- **TRIUMPH-1 (Phase 3):** 70.3 lbs (28.3%) average weight loss at 80 weeks on 12mg. BMI ≥35 patients lost up to 85 lbs (30.3%) at 104 weeks.
- **TRIUMPH-4 (Phase 3):** 28.7% at 68 weeks
- 45.3% of participants achieved ≥30% weight loss — a threshold historically only achievable with bariatric surgery
- **Beyond weight loss:** 75.8% reduction in osteoarthritis pain, ~20% LDL cholesterol reduction, 72% prediabetes reversal rate
- **Timeline:** Seven additional Phase 3 readouts expected in 2026. Not yet approved anywhere. Filing likely 2027, potential approval 2028.
- **Investment Implication:** This is why LLY commands a premium multiple. Retatrutide could be a $20B+ peak sales drug if approved.

### CagriSema — Novo's Counter-Punch

- NDA submitted to FDA December 2025 (REDEFINE 1 + 2 data)
- REIMAGINE 2 (Phase 3): Superior HbA1c and weight loss vs. semaglutide alone
- Higher-dose trial (CagriSema 2.4/2.4mg) starting H2 2026; REDEFINE 11 readout expected H1 2027
- If approved, gives Novo a next-gen injectable to compete with tirzepatide

### Muscle-Sparing Approaches — The Next Frontier

~40% of weight lost on GLP-1 drugs comes from muscle mass. This is a clinical concern and a massive commercial opportunity.

- **Bimagrumab (Lilly/Versanis):** Acquired for ~$2B. BELIEVE trial showed 22.1% weight loss with 92.8% from fat at 72 weeks. BUT: elevated LDL, liver enzymes, muscle spasms, acne. One Phase 2 trial terminated Sept 2025 for "strategic reasons."
- **Apitegromab (Scholar Rock):** Selective myostatin inhibitor. EMBRAZE Phase 2 + tirzepatide showed 85% of weight loss from fat with no serious adverse events. Cleaner safety profile than bimagrumab.
- **Biohaven:** Betting on muscle-focused approach to weight loss as differentiated entry.
- **Investment Angle:** Whoever solves the muscle-loss problem creates a combination therapy that becomes standard of care. Scholar Rock and Biohaven are small-cap plays on this thesis.

## 7. Compounding Pharmacy Disruption

### The Regulatory Crackdown

The compounding GLP-1 market grew rapidly during the semaglutide/tirzepatide shortage period (2023-2025). The FDA is now systematically shutting it down.

**Key Actions:**
- **March 2026:** FDA issued 50+ warning letters in a single enforcement wave to telehealth firms and compounding pharmacies
- **June 2026:** FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, AND liraglutide from the 503B outsourcing facility bulk drug substances list. Comment period closes June 29, 2026.
- **Enforcement rationale:** Unauthorized compounding after shortage resolution, misleading marketing, improper manufacturing
- **Current rules:** Only FDA-registered 503B facilities and state-licensed 503A pharmacies (individual prescriptions) may compound under specific conditions

### Hims & Hers (HIMS) — The Canary in the Coal Mine

| Metric | Detail |
|---|---|
| **2025 Revenue** | $2.35B (+57% YoY) |
| **2026 Revenue Guidance** | $2.7B-$2.9B |
| **Compounded Semaglutide Price** | ~$49/month |
| **FDA Warning Letters** | September 2025 |
| **DOJ Referral** | February 2026 — HHS referred HIMS to DOJ for potential FDCA violations |
| **Novo Relationship** | Severed — Novo terminated partnership citing "illegal mass compounding and deceptive marketing" |

**Impact on Branded Drug Makers:**
- **Positive for LLY and NVO** — Compounding crackdown removes low-cost alternative, driving patients to branded products
- **Lilly's Foundayo at $149/month** and **Novo's oral Wegovy at $149/month** are priced to compete directly with compounders
- The pricing strategy is deliberate — make the branded oral product cheap enough that compounding loses its economic rationale

## Investment Thesis Summary

### The Framework

**Social Arbitrage:** The market still underestimates total addressable indications. Every new label (Alzheimer's, addiction, osteoarthritis) is an upside surprise the street hasn't fully priced. Retail investors see "weight loss drugs." Institutional capital sees a platform drug class.

**Infinite Game:** Lilly is playing the infinite game — $55B in manufacturing, pipeline sequenced (Mounjaro → Foundayo → retatrutide), pricing set to kill compounders. Novo is playing defense.

**AI Disruption / Moat Analysis:** GLP-1 moats are built on (1) clinical data depth, (2) manufacturing scale, (3) label breadth, and (4) oral formulation IP. Lilly has all four. Novo has 1 and partially 2. Everyone else is catching up.

## 14. Compounding Pharmacy Regulatory Impact

### Timeline of Crackdown

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Oct 2024** | Tirzepatide shortage declared resolved |
| **Feb 2025** | Semaglutide shortage declared resolved |
| **Sep 2025** | FDA issues warning letters to Hims & Hers |
| **Feb 2026** | HHS refers Hims & Hers to DOJ; FDA announces enforcement priorities |
| **Mar 2026** | 50+ warning letters to telehealth firms and compounders |
| **Apr 2026** | FDA clarifies 503A safe harbor: max 4 Rx per compound per month |
| **May 2026** | FDA proposes excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, AND liraglutide from 503B bulks list |
| **June 29, 2026** | Comment period closes on 503B exclusion proposal |

### Impact Assessment

- Compounded GLP-1s reached ~30% of US supply at peak (2024)
- Both Lilly ($149/mo Foundayo) and Novo ($149/mo oral Wegovy) priced oral pills to undercut compounders
- FDA enforcement is systematically eliminating the compounding channel
- **Net positive for LLY and NVO** — removes low-cost alternative and drives patients to branded products
- **Hims & Hers (HIMS):** Stock highly volatile; $22.45 in March 2026; partnership with Novo to distribute branded products but at lower margins than compounding; Q1 2026 revenue miss, -$0.40 EPS vs. $0.03 expected. Avoid.
