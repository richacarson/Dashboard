
# SpaceX IPO & 2026 IPO Wave — July Rebalance Positioning

## Thesis

The largest IPO in stock market history prices on June 11, 2026: **SpaceX (SPCX)** at **$135/share**, raising **$75 billion** at a **$1.75 trillion** post-money valuation. This is not a normal IPO — it is a gravitational event that will restructure index composition, force ~$49B in passive selling of existing S&P 500 constituents, and absorb capital from every corner of the market. Combined with **OpenAI** (~$852B, Sept 2026), **Anthropic** (~$965B, Fall 2026), and **Cerebras** (already listed, $56B), the 2026 IPO class could absorb **$200B+ in fresh capital** in six months. Goldman Sachs projects 2026 IPO proceeds at ~$160B — a 4x increase from 2025. For Family Capital's late July rebalance, the question is not whether to buy SpaceX day one (we shouldn't), but how the capital rotation mechanics affect our existing 52 holdings and when the best entry points materialize.

## Historical IPO Wave Lessons

### What Happens to Existing Holdings

| Mega-IPO | What Happened to Peers/Market |
|---|---|
| **Facebook (2012)** | Took 16 months to recover IPO price. Social media peers initially sold off, then rallied as the sector got validated |
| **Alibaba (2014)** | Pulled capital from other e-commerce names short-term. S&P 500 +5% in the month after |
| **Uber/Lyft (2019)** | Both fell 30%+ from IPO within 6 months. Didn't drag the broader market |
| **Rivian (2021)** | Hit $172B market cap on day 3, then collapsed 88% from peak. Auto sector peers unaffected long-term |

**Key finding (Goldman Sachs):** The S&P 500 typically rallies ~5% in the month *after* mega-IPOs. The risk is not the IPOs themselves but the mechanics of forced index rebalancing.

### IPO Performance Statistics

- **64% of IPOs underperform the S&P 500** in their first year
- Average first-day pop of 18% fades to just 3% by month 12
- Buying at IPO price beats buying on day 1 by an average of 15%
- Lock-up expiry creates 2-10% average declines — the best entry point

## The Bottom Line — July Rebalance Playbook

### What We're Doing

1. **Buying the index rebalancing dip in AI semis (NVDA, AMD, TSM, MRVL, CRDO, LRCX).** SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 inclusion forces ~$49B in selling of existing constituents. Our semiconductor holdings will get sold mechanically regardless of fundamentals. This is a buying opportunity — AI compute demand has never been more validated (Anthropic at $44B annualized, Cerebras profitable, CoreWeave tripling revenue)

2. **Holding defense (GD, LMT).** SpaceX's $22B in government contracts validates the defense/space spending trajectory. Defense stocks outperformed during pre-IPO space sector volatility. $901B defense budget provides a fundamental floor

3. **Evaluating Cerebras (CBRS) for Growth Hybrid sleeve.** The only profitable pure-play AI chip company. Post-IPO volatility may create an entry in the $210-250 range. Fits our AI infrastructure theme

4. **Reducing consumer credit exposure (SYF).** Klarna's post-IPO collapse (-59%) confirms consumer credit stress. SYF was already flagged for reduction in oil shock research. This reinforces the signal

5. **NOT buying SpaceX at IPO.** 70x revenue, negative earnings, Morningstar says 55% overvalued. The staggered lock-up creates entry windows in Sept-Dec 2026. If we decide SpaceX belongs in the portfolio, December is the time — not June

6. **NOT chasing any 2026 IPO in the first 30 days.** 64% underperform the S&P in year one. The first-day pop averages 18% but fades to 3% by month 12. Lock-up expiry is the institutional entry point

### What We're Watching

- **Brent crude + SpaceX + index rebalancing** — three forces hitting simultaneously in late June/July. The oil shock research flagged Q4 2026 as the earnings impact zone. If SpaceX index rebalancing causes a broad pullback that coincides with early oil shock earnings misses, we could see a sharper correction than either event alone
- **OpenAI and Anthropic IPO pricing** — if both come at $800B+, the combined capital absorption with SpaceX could tighten market liquidity significantly in Q4 2026
- **Mutual fund cash at 5th percentile historically** ($181B, 1.4% of assets) — there is very little dry powder to absorb this IPO wave without selling existing positions

### Present to Committee

Frame this as: **The 2026 IPO wave validates our AI infrastructure thesis but the capital absorption mechanics create tactical opportunities in names we already own.** The right move is not to chase new IPOs — it's to add to NVDA, AMD, TSM, MRVL, CRDO, and LRCX when index rebalancing forces mechanical selling. SpaceX is a December conversation, not a June one.

## Risks to This Analysis

1. **SpaceX rips 50%+ from IPO and we miss it.** Jim Cramer predicts $4T. If Starlink subscriber growth accelerates and xAI losses narrow, the bull case is real. But at 70x revenue, the risk/reward isn't there on day one
2. **Index rebalancing dip doesn't materialize.** If retail demand for SpaceX is so strong that it pulls in net new capital (rather than rotating from existing holdings), our semiconductor buying opportunity may not appear
3. **OpenAI/Anthropic IPOs get delayed.** If these push to 2027, the capital absorption problem is smaller and market liquidity stays tighter for longer
4. **SpaceX lock-up selling is lighter than expected.** The staggered structure may reduce the typical lock-up dip. Insiders who believe in $4T+ may hold
5. **Ceasefire rally.** If the Iran situation resolves, risk-on rotation could lift all boats and make the tactical positioning less relevant
6. **Consumer credit is fine.** If Klarna's problems are company-specific (not macro), our SYF reduction is premature
