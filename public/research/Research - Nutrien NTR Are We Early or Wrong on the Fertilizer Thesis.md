
# Nutrien (NTR) — Are We Early or Wrong on the Fertilizer Thesis?

## Thesis

We added NTR to the Dividend portfolio a couple months ago on the Persian Gulf fertilizer crisis thesis — 30-35% of global ammonia production offline, Strait of Hormuz closed, QAFCO (14% of global urea trade) shut down. The stock has pulled back from its crisis highs and we're underwater. The question: are we early, or has the market already priced this in and moved on?

**Verdict: The crisis trade already happened — and we missed the window.** Ammonia prices spiked from $650/mt to $775/mt in March-April, then round-tripped back to ~$392/mt by June — the same level as June 2025. Urea hit $453.50/st on June 6, the lowest since before the war started. Bloomberg's headline on June 9: *"Crop and Fertilizer Markets Wipe Out Iran War Risk Premium."* The market is front-running a US-Iran deal even though diplomacy remains fragile.

NTR surged from the mid-$50s to $85, then gave it all back to $66. We bought near the top. The honest assessment: the initial trade thesis played out exactly as predicted (March-April), and we caught the backside. CF Industries was up 76% by mid-March and has since pulled back 20%. The pure-play nitrogen trade was the right call — we just had the wrong vehicle (diversified NTR) and wrong timing (post-spike instead of pre-spike).

**But this is not a total loss.** The Strait remains closed. QAFCO remains offline. Russia and China are still restricting exports. The 60-day US-Iran MOU framework is NOT finalized — Iran broke off talks on June 1. Any re-escalation immediately re-ignites the trade. NTR beat Q1 estimates, posted record potash volumes, ran nitrogen at 92% utilization, and yields 3.3%. At $66 with a $82 average analyst PT, there's still 23% upside on fundamentals alone — before any crisis re-pricing. The question for committee: hold for the structural value + optionality on deal collapse, or cut losses and redeploy?

## The Fertilizer Crisis — Status Update (June 2026)

### Strait of Hormuz — STILL CLOSED (But a Deal Framework Exists)

The physical disruption remains, but the diplomatic picture has evolved significantly:

- **Closure date:** March 4, 2026 — now 3+ months
- **Maritime transit:** Dropped to approximately zero. 20,000 mariners and 2,000 ships stranded
- **QAFCO:** Offline since March 2. Storage capacity exhausted within 4-6 weeks. QatarEnergy declared force majeure
- **Persian Gulf fertilizer production:** 30-35% of global urea exports and 20-30% of ammonia exports remain offline
- **Restart timeline:** Even if deal is signed tomorrow, 6+ months for LNG restart, longer for chemical plants

**Diplomatic Timeline:**
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **Feb 28** | US/Israel launched Operation Epic Fury. Iran's Khamenei killed |
| **Mar 4** | Iran declared Strait "closed." Traffic dropped to near-zero |
| **Apr 8** | Two-week ceasefire agreed (Pakistan-mediated). Shipping remained at standstill |
| **Apr 13** | US initiated counter-blockade after talks collapsed |
| **Apr 17** | Iran announced Strait would reopen during Israel-Lebanon truce. Urea fell 18% on the news |
| **May 24-29** | **60-day US-Iran MOU reached** — Strait to be "unrestricted," mines removed in 30 days, US blockade lifted proportionally. Awaiting Trump's final approval |
| **Jun 1** | **Iran broke off negotiations**, vowed to "completely" block the Strait |
| **Jun 9** | Contradictory signals. Markets pricing in resolution; diplomacy is fragile |

**Key: The market is front-running a deal that doesn't exist yet.** The 60-day MOU framework is real but unsigned. Iran's June 1 walkout is real. Any re-escalation re-ignites the trade instantly.

### Supply Restrictions Compounding the Crisis

## Why We're Down — The Pullback Explained

NTR surged from the mid-$50s to ~$85 when the Hormuz crisis broke in March. It has since pulled back to ~$67, and we bought somewhere near the top. Here's what happened:

### 1. "Buy the Rumor, Sell the News" — The Crisis Premium Faded

The initial surge priced in maximum disruption. As weeks passed without global famine headlines, some of the panic premium came out. Markets are impatient — the fertilizer thesis is a multi-quarter/multi-year structural shift, but momentum traders wanted immediate price action.

### 2. New US Capacity Moderated the Panic

The Gulf Coast Ammonia and Woodside plants coming online gave the market a narrative that "the US can backfill." This is directionally true but quantitatively wrong — 2.3 MT of new capacity doesn't replace 55-60 MT offline. But it was enough to take the edge off the panic bid.

### 3. NTR's Diversification Dilutes the Nitrogen Story

This is the structural issue: NTR is not a pure nitrogen play. Its four segments — Retail, Potash, Nitrogen, Phosphate — mean the nitrogen tailwind is diluted. The Retail segment ($108M EBITDA in a slow Q1) is a different business entirely — it's crop input distribution, sensitive to farmer economics and inventory cycles. CF Industries captures 100% of the nitrogen tailwind. NTR captures maybe 40-45%.

### 4. Markets Front-Running a US-Iran Deal

This is the biggest factor. The 60-day MOU framework negotiated in late May gave markets confidence that the Strait would reopen. Even though Iran broke off talks June 1, the market is still pricing in an eventual resolution. Fertilizer prices have round-tripped to pre-war levels — ammonia from $775 to $392, urea from $700+ to $453. Both CF and NTR hit their lowest since March.

### 5. Broader Market Headwinds

S&P 500 is -4% YTD. Risk-off sentiment in materials and cyclicals. The bear market risk assessment we wrote in April (29-49% recession probability) is weighing on cyclical names like NTR.

### 6. Seasonal Demand Peak Passing

Northern Hemisphere spring application season — the period of peak fertilizer demand — is ending. Spring planting is 87% complete. The urgency to secure fertilizer supply has faded for this growing season.

## Risks

1. **Ceasefire.** If Hormuz reopens and Gulf production restarts, the nitrogen premium collapses. NTR drops 15-20%, CF drops 25-30%. This is the primary risk to the thesis. Timeline: restart is 6+ months from ceasefire, so even a deal doesn't immediately normalize supply.

2. **New US capacity exceeds expectations.** If Gulf Coast Ammonia, Woodside, and potential additional plants come online faster, domestic nitrogen supply improves faster than expected. Mitigant: 2.3 MT doesn't replace 55-60 MT.

3. **Demand destruction.** If fertilizer prices stay too high, developing-world farmers use less. Reduces demand but also reduces food production — humanitarian crisis, not market rebalancing.

4. **US export controls.** If the crisis deepens, the US government could restrict fertilizer exports to protect domestic food security. This caps upside for NTR and CF by limiting their ability to sell at international premium prices.

5. **NTR Retail segment underperformance.** The retail business is sensitive to farmer credit, inventory cycles, and weather. A bad growing season could compress retail margins independently of the nitrogen thesis.

6. **Cyclical valuation compression.** Fertilizer stocks are cyclical. If the market prices this as peak earnings, multiples compress even while fundamentals improve.

7. **CF-specific: DOJ antitrust probe.** CF Industries faces a Department of Justice antitrust investigation regarding pricing behavior during the supply shortage. If this results in enforcement action, it could cap CF's ability to benefit from elevated nitrogen prices. Worth monitoring.

8. **Belarusian potash sanctions removal.** Recent removal of Belarusian potash sanctions creates potential supply increase that pressures NTR's potash pricing power (~37% of EBITDA). This is NTR-specific downside risk that doesn't affect CF.

9. **BHP Jansen Mine — the biggest long-term threat to NTR's potash business.** Stage 1 is 75% complete, first production mid-2027. 4.15MT/year capacity — roughly 20% of Nutrien's potash volume. Stage 2 approved (4.36MT), targeting 2031. Total Jansen capacity of 8.5MT = ~10% increase in global potash supply. This is the single most important competitive threat to NTR this decade.

10. **UBS bear case (Sell rating, March 26).** UBS sees potash pricing declining from Q2 2026, keeping FY2026 flat vs. consensus expecting increases. Their 2028 EBITDA estimate is 4% below consensus — trajectory matters more than current quarter. Combined with shorter-than-expected nitrogen disruption, UBS sees downside.

9. **Corn acreage shift.** At current fertilizer prices, many farmers face projected net losses of $210+/acre for corn. US corn acreage could drop by 5M acres with flex acres rotating to soybeans (less nitrogen-intensive). This is demand destruction that could moderate nitrogen prices in 2027.
