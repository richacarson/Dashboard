### SELL · EIX — Edison International · Screener score 64

- **Why we sold:** EIX could not fundamentally handle the wildfire liability it faces. This was a fundamentals decision.
- **The numbers:** Potential full liability of over $13B against $240M current cash, while at the same time debt to equity has been steadily increasing (2.5 versus 1.0 in 2016) and the company has had negative free cash flow since 2018.

### BUY · PGY — Pagaya Technologies · Screener score 66

- **What it does:** Pagaya evaluates consumer credit applications for banks and fintech lenders like SoFi, Klarna, Visa and others, approving borrowers that traditional credit scores would turn away. About 1 out of every 100 passes, turning a profit out of what otherwise would have been a missed opportunity. It plugs into partners by API and earns fees on the loans it helps originate, across personal loans, auto, credit cards, buy-now-pay-later and real estate.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 8.6x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 5-year median P/E of 21.11x. FCF yield of 14.3% is the highest in the company's public history.

**Long term yields are at their highest levels in decades and the Fed has moved from holding to hiking.**

| Indicator | Reading |

### What this means for positioning

- **Rates are the common thread in the sells.** The 10-year at 5.2% works against PCAR and TOL. For TOL, even though its homes are luxury, roughly 25% of buyers pay cash, so about 75% still finance, at a 30-year mortgage rate near 7.40%, while the general housing market is weak. NEE spends roughly $40B a year on capex and has a funding gap of about $29B a year, which it closes by returning to the capital markets every year rather than with a single loan, so higher yields keep feeding into its cost of capital.
- **The buys have accelerating demand or pricing power.** AVGO, VRT and GNRC are each expected to see sharply higher demand over the next few years: Broadcom's AI revenue guidance steps from about $58B to $115B to $230B, Vertiv has a $15.0B backlog, and Generac has about $1.6B of backlog. WM and YOU each have a moat that lets them raise prices.

## 04 · Proposed Dividend Portfolio Changes

### Exits

#### SELL · PCAR — PACCAR · Screener score 55

- **Why now:** The 10-year at 5.2% raises customer financing costs and the discount rate on the recovery.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 18.1x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 14.87x. EV/EBITDA is 18.7x versus 10.1x historical, the highest in its record. Gross margin is 16.2%, the lowest in 12 years, and operating margin is 10.4% versus 16.9% in 2023. Revenue peaked at $35.1B in 2023 and fell to $28.4B in 2025, and consensus does not get back above $35B until 2030. The stock price though, is $108 versus $97.65 at the end of 2023.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Revenue peaked in 2023 and is not expected to get back above 2023 levels until 2030, and gross margin has compressed to its lowest in 12 years, while at the same time the stock trades about 85% above its own historical EV/EBITDA (18.7x versus 10.1x).

- **Tax:** Selling PCAR realizes −$132,092 short-term and +$411,981 long-term, for a net gain of $279,889. The average account's gain on its cost basis is +8.8%.

#### SELL · NEE — NextEra Energy · Screener score 78

- **Why now:** Opportunity cost with AVGO. NEE is still a good company, but it trades as a bond proxy, needs roughly $29B a year of external funding, and the 10-year at 5.2% is a direct headwind.
- **Numbers:** From FY2020 to FY2025 total debt grew 14.7% a year versus 8.8% for revenue, and the share count has risen every year since FY2014 (1.76B to 2.09B).

> **Numbers in plain terms:** The company is borrowing faster than it is growing, has issued more shares every year for over a decade, and still needs about $29B a year of outside money, so each rate increase lands directly on its cost of funding.

- **Tax:** Selling NEE realizes −$25,350 short-term and +$448,926 long-term, for a net gain of $423,576. The average account's gain on its cost basis is +3.2%.

#### SELL · CHD — Church & Dwight · Screener score 62

- **Why now:** Opportunity cost with WM.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 32.0x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 28.65x. EV/EBITDA is 20.6x versus 19.2x historical. Consensus EPS growth is about 6% a year. Operating margin is 17.4% versus a 20.7% peak in 2016, and EBITDA margin is 20.9% versus 24.1%. Over the last five years revenue grew 4.8% a year while EPS fell 0.6% a year.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Sales are growing under 5% a year and earnings per share have shrunk, margins are below where they were ten years ago, and to earn 15% a year the market would have to pay about 12% more for those earnings in 2029 than it has historically.

- **Tax:** Selling CHD realizes +$74,547 short-term and +$969 long-term, for a net gain of $75,516. The average account's gain on its cost basis is +3.9%.

### Additions

#### BUY · YOU — Clear Secure · Screener score 72

- **What it does:** CLEAR charges travelers an annual fee, paid upfront (raised to $219 July 1), to verify their identity with a fingerprint, eye or face scan so they skip the ID-check line at airport security. It also earns TSA PreCheck enrollment fees and sells a business identity-verification platform (CLEAR1).
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 18.3x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own median P/E of 29.83x since its 2021 IPO. Free-cash-flow yield of 12.2% is near the highest it has been in its public history. Consensus EPS growth is about 25% a year. Operating margin has risen every year since FY2021 and EBITDA margin over the same span. The company is also net cash.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Earnings are expected to grow about 25% a year, margins have risen every year since FY2021, and the company holds net cash.

#### BUY · AVGO — Broadcom · Screener score 81

- **What it does:** Broadcom designs custom AI chips and the networking chips that wire them together for a handful of the world's largest AI builders, sells standard chips to everyone else, and rents VMware and other data-center software to big enterprises on multi-year subscriptions.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 11.5x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 17.03x. It trades at 19.5x FY27E earnings (Oct 6 price), with EPS expected to rise from $11.68 (FY26E) to $19.30 and $30.65 (FY28E), about 2.6x in two years. Gross, operating and EBITDA margins have risen since FY2024.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Earnings per share are expected to rise about 2.6x in two years ($11.68 to $30.65) with margins rising since FY2024, and to earn 15% a year the stock only has to trade at 11.5x versus a 17.03x median, about 33% below its usual multiple.

#### BUY · WM — Waste Management · Screener score 73

- **What it does:** WM is North America's largest trash company. It charges homes, businesses and towns a recurring fee to haul trash away, then charges again to bury it in landfills it owns, and raises prices a little faster than inflation every year. Since buying Stericycle in late 2024 it also collects medical waste.
- **Moat:** Landfill permits are close to impossible to replace, so WM can raise prices ahead of its own cost inflation year after year.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 28.7x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 27.99x. EV/EBITDA is 14.1x versus 15.0x historical, and free-cash-flow yield is 4.3% versus a 3.5% median, above every year-end since FY2018. Consensus EPS growth is about 9.6% a year, at about 23x FY27 earnings. Gross margin rose every year from 26.9% in FY2021 to 29.1% in FY2024 and held in FY2025.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Earnings are growing about 9.6% a year, free cash flow yield (4.3%) is above its 3.5% median, and the stock needs roughly its normal multiple (28.7x versus a 27.99x median) to earn 15% a year.

## 05 · Proposed Growth Portfolio Changes

### Exits

#### SELL · MARA — Marathon Digital · Screener score 50

- **Why now:** We have waited too long for MARA to become profitable and conviction in the company has lessened over time.
- **Numbers:** Consensus EPS is negative through 2029. Consensus has revenue falling 16% in 2026 ($0.91B to $0.76B) and no profit through 2029.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Analysts expect revenue to fall 16% this year and no profit at all through 2029, so there are no earnings to value, and the business has been losing money for longer than we are willing to wait.

- **Tax:** Selling MARA realizes −$74,154 short-term and $0 long-term, for a net loss of $74,154. The average account's gain on its cost basis is −8.3%.

#### SELL · TOL — Toll Brothers · Screener score 59

- **Why now:** Opportunity cost with GNRC and 10-year above 5% squeezes mortgage affordability and margins, and the housing market is slow.
- **Numbers:** Free-cash-flow yield is 8.5% versus an 11.8% median. Consensus has revenue falling 1.5% in FY2026, then rising only 1.9% in FY2027. In FY25 EPS fell 10% and EBITDA 14%, and operating margin slipped from 18.8% to 15.7%. Free cash flow is $1.03B versus $0.93B in 2017, and net income of $1.35B is below FY2023 ($1.37B) and 14% under the FY2024 peak ($1.57B).

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Revenue is expected to shrink this year and barely grow next year, profit is already 14% below its 2024 peak with margins falling from 18.8% to 15.7%, and about three of every four buyers need a mortgage near 7.4%.

- **Tax:** Selling TOL realizes −$12,060 short-term and +$27,163 long-term, for a net gain of $15,103. The average account's gain on its cost basis is +2.9%.

### Additions

#### BUY · VRT — Vertiv · Screener score 80

- **What it does:** Vertiv builds, sells and services the power and cooling equipment that keeps data-center servers running. It earns a hardware margin on every megawatt of capacity built and a service income stream on the equipment already installed. The bottleneck in AI has moved to power delivery and cooling between the substation and the rack.
- **The backlog:** Vertiv made $10.23B of revenue in all of 2025 and $5.92B in the first half of 2026, and guides to $13.8–14.2B for 2026. The backlog is therefore about 1.5 years of 2025 revenue and roughly equal to a full year at the 2026 guide.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 27.7x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 32.51x. Consensus EPS growth is about 25% a year. Operating margin has risen every year since FY2022 (4.0% to 18.5%) and EBITDA margin every year since FY2020 (2.3% to 20.9%). Last reporting period, the company had $3.1B of cash and short-term investments against $3.3B of total debt ($2.9B excluding leases). The stock traded about 34% below its 52-week high in mid-September while hyperscaler capex is up roughly 80% year over year.

> **Numbers in plain terms:** Earnings are expected to grow about 25% a year on a $15.0B order backlog, margins have risen every year for several years, and to earn 15% a year the stock needs 27.7x versus a 32.51x median, about 15% below its usual multiple.

#### BUY · GNRC — Generac · Screener score 67

- **What it does:** Generac builds backup generators and earns a manufacturing margin on each, plus a smaller stream from parts, service and extended warranties. Most units are home standby generators sold through local installing dealers; a fast-growing share are multi-megawatt units for data centers, including a supply deal signed with Amazon on Sept 16, 2026, with initial deliveries of about $2.4B across 2027–28.
- **Numbers:** Must-believe exit multiple of 19.8x for a 15% annual CAGR through 2029, versus the company's own 10-year median P/E of 20.57x. 2027 consensus has revenue up 32% and EPS up 33%. Operating margin recovered from −0.9% in Q4 2025 to 11.1% in Q1 2026 and 17.9% in Q2 2026 (10.5% a year earlier), and gross margin was 44.5% in Q2 2026 versus 39.3%. Total debt fell from $1.73B (FY2023) to $1.33B (FY2025).

> **Numbers in plain terms:** 2027 consensus has revenue up 32% and earnings up 33% with margins rebounding sharply, and to earn 15% a year the stock needs 19.8x versus a 20.57x median, about 4% below its usual multiple.
