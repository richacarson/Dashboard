# Solar or Nuclear? Reviewing the CEG and VST Thesis, and Where a 2× Actually Lives in Solar

*Research note — 18 September 2026. Prepared in response to two questions: whether holding Constellation Energy and Vistra is the right expression of the data-centre power thesis, and whether the sleeve needs solar exposure alongside it. Pre-tax. Prices as of the 18 September session.*

---

## Summary

**The nuclear and gas positions are right, and they are cheaper than when we bought them.** CEG and VST are down 38% and 36% from their highs, but the decline is a re-rating of the whole independent-power complex on softer ERCOT forward curves — not a failure of the contracts. Those contracts are intact and long-dated.

**Solar the technology is winning. Solar the equity has been a value trap for fifteen years, and the reason is visible in this year's numbers.** The complex is down 40–67% because Chinese oversupply has commoditised manufacturing and because US policy is being withdrawn on a hard schedule. The low multiples are not a mispricing; they are a discount for a risk that is real.

**One name survives the screen on quality, and it comes with a caveat large enough to state twice.**

---

## Part 1 — Are CEG and VST the right holdings?

Yes, and the thesis has not broken. What has happened is a price move, and the cause is identifiable.

| | Price | Off 52-wk high | Vs 200-day | Forward P/E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CEG (6.0% target) | $254.71 | −38.3% | −13% | **12.9× 2029E** |
| VST (6.0% target) | $140.67 | −36.0% | −10% | **9.9× 2029E** |

VST's consensus EPS goes $10.22 (2027) → $16.13 (2030), a **16.4% annual growth rate priced at 9.9× three-year-forward earnings**. That combination does not normally coexist.

**Why they fell.** The data-centre-linked IPPs compressed together as ERCOT forward curves softened toward ~$30/MWh. VST's Q2 revenue slipped 5.5% to $4.02B and GAAP earnings absorbed $472M of unrealised mark-to-market hedge losses — an accounting effect on hedges, not a cash loss. This was a complex-wide de-rating, not a company-specific break.

**What did not change.** Vistra holds a 20-year PPA with AWS for 1,200MW from Comanche Peak, Meta PPAs covering more than 2,600MW across PJM nuclear sites, and the Helix Digital Infrastructure JV with NVIDIA, KKR and the Kuwait Investment Authority, with up to $1.0B committed and VST as preferred power provider. Those are contracted, multi-decade, investment-grade counterparties.

**The structural argument.** Data centres need firm, dispatchable, 24/7 power. That is the entire requirement. Solar is the cheapest marginal electron on earth and cannot supply it without storage that does not yet exist at that scale or price. Every hyperscaler signing nuclear PPAs at a premium to grid power is telling you they have already run this analysis. Holding nuclear and gas generation against data-centre load growth is the correct expression of the thesis, and the softer ERCOT curve is a cyclical input to a structural trade.

**Conclusion: hold both, and treat the current price as an addition point rather than an exit.** The risk to watch is not contract failure; it is ERCOT forwards staying near $30 long enough to reset the merchant tail beyond the contracted volumes.

## Part 2 — Is solar the future, and does the sleeve need it?

Solar will be the largest source of new global generating capacity. That is not in dispute and it is also not the question. The question is whether owning solar *equities* captures it, and the fifteen-year record says the value leaks out of the manufacturing layer almost entirely.

**Why the complex is down 40–67%:**

1. **Chinese oversupply.** China manufactures more than 80% of the world's panels, and domestic competition has produced a glut. Panel prices are low, which is wonderful for electricity consumers and fatal for module margins.
2. **A US policy cliff with a date on it.** Under OBBBA, wind and solar projects beginning construction after 4 July 2026 must be placed in service by **end-2027** to qualify. IRS Notice 2025-42 restricts the old 5% cost safe harbor to small facilities, so larger projects must now demonstrate physical work to lock eligibility. That is a pull-forward followed by a hole.
3. **Credit dependence.** First Solar's own 2026 guidance embeds **$2.10–2.19B of Section 45X manufacturing tax credits**. On a company whose consensus 2026 net income is a fraction above that, the credit is not a kicker — it is the earnings.
4. An ongoing ITC investigation into certain solar products, and net insider selling at FSLR.

That is why the sector is cheap. It is cheap for cause.

### The 2× screen

Required exit multiple on year+3 consensus EPS for the stock to double from today:

| | Layer | Price | Mkt cap | Fwd P/E (yr+3) | **Multiple needed to 2×** | EPS trend |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **FSLR** | Thin-film modules | $195.96 | $21.1B | 5.7× (2029) | **11.5×** | rising |
| **ARRY** | Trackers | $4.06 | $0.6B | 4.7× (2029) | **9.3×** | **peaks then falls** |
| **SHLS** | Electrical BOS | $7.60 | $1.3B | 9.6× (2029) | 19.2× | rising |
| **NXT** | Trackers | $80.47 | $12.2B | 10.2× (FY30) | 20.5× | rising |
| **ENPH** | Microinverters | $34.64 | $4.6B | 10.7× (2029) | 21.3× | rising |

**Read the third and fourth columns together.** ARRY needs only a 9.3× exit multiple to double — the lowest bar on the board — but consensus has its EPS *peaking in 2028 and declining thereafter* on flat revenue, and the stock closed at its 52-week low today. A low required multiple on a declining earnings stream is not an opportunity; it is the definition of a value trap. **Do not buy ARRY on this screen.**

**FSLR is the only name where the arithmetic and the business quality point the same way.** It needs 11.5× on 2029 estimates to double — a multiple it has exceeded in every good year it has had. It is the only US-scale manufacturer with a non-Chinese supply chain, which is precisely what the tariff and ITC environment rewards.

**And here is the caveat, stated plainly: a majority of that EPS is a tax credit.** Strip or shrink 45X and the $34 of 2029 earnings is not $34. The estimate dispersion says the Street knows this — 2028 consensus EPS runs from $23.84 to $40.22, a 69% spread. **FSLR is not a cheap manufacturer. It is a leveraged bet on US industrial policy surviving the next two elections, priced at 5.7× if it does.** Size it as the policy bet it is, not as a value stock.

SHLS, NXT and ENPH all need roughly 19–21× to double. That is achievable in a recovery but requires both multiple expansion and the demand cliff not materialising. They are second-order candidates at best.

### Where solar value actually accrues

If the committee wants exposure to the solar *build* rather than the solar *manufacture*, the money has historically been made one layer away from the panel — in the grid equipment that connects it and the developers that own the output. **GE Vernova ($940, already held in the FCI sleeve) is the cleanest expression**: turbines, grid equipment and electrification, sold into the same load growth without the Chinese commodity exposure or the 45X dependency. It is not cheap, but it is not a policy derivative either.

## Recommendation

1. **Hold CEG and VST at 6.0% each.** The thesis is intact, the contracts are contracted, and the de-rating is an ERCOT curve problem rather than a demand problem. VST at 9.9× 2029E with 16% earnings growth is the better of the two on valuation.
2. **No general solar allocation.** The sector's cheapness is compensation for Chinese oversupply and a dated US policy cliff, not a mispricing.
3. **If the committee wants a solar 2×, the only defensible vehicle is FSLR, sized as a policy bet** — a small, deliberate position, not a core holding. Set it against an explicit view on 45X.
4. **Avoid ARRY** despite it screening cheapest. Declining consensus EPS, flat revenue, a 52-week low and a $625M market cap is a falling knife, not a discount.
5. **For the "solar is the future" exposure without the solar equity problem, GE Vernova already in the FCI sleeve is the better instrument.**

**What would change the call:** clarity on 45X beyond 2027 would make FSLR a straightforward buy rather than a policy bet. A sustained recovery in ERCOT forwards above ~$40/MWh would make the IPP positions worth adding to aggressively.
