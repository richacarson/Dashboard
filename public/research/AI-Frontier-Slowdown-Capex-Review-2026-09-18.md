# Does Slowing the Frontier Slow the Buildout? Testing the Case for Cutting AI Weight

*Research note — 18 September 2026. Prepared in response to the 12 September calls by Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI to slow frontier model development, and a proposal to reduce weight in the AI complex on that basis. Pre-tax. Prices as of the 18 September session.*

---

## Summary

**The news does not support cutting AI weight, because it is not news about compute demand.** Three labs proposed slowing the rate at which model *capability* advances. None of them owns the buildout. The $725 billion being spent on data centres in 2026 is being spent by Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft — none of whom signed the pledge, none of whom has changed guidance, and none of whom is building primarily to train frontier models.

There is a good case for trimming parts of this book. It rests on valuation and position sizing, not on this announcement.

## What was actually said

On 12 September, Dario Amodei published an essay calling for an immediate slowdown in the pace of frontier development, warning that within 6–12 months swarms of rogue agents could cause damage in the hundreds of billions. Sam Altman agreed within a day, adding that an IPO would be "ill-advised" now and "not 2026". Elon Musk agreed publicly. The proposal itself is three things: independent reviewers inside the labs, coordination on safety standards between frontier firms, and international cooperation.

Read the list again. Every item is about **governance of capability**. Not one is about deploying less compute. A model that is reviewed more carefully before release still has to be served to users, and serving is where the compute goes.

## Who actually spends the money

This is the part that decides the question.

| Hyperscaler | 2026 capex guidance |
|---|---:|
| Amazon | ~$220B |
| Google | $195–205B |
| Microsoft | ~$175B |
| Meta | $130–145B |
| **Combined** | **~$720–745B** |

That is up from roughly $410 billion in 2025 — an **~80% increase** — and approximately 75% of it is AI-related. As of the latest guidance round, none of it has been revised down. Investors sold Google, Amazon and Microsoft after those calls precisely *because* the spending was maintained.

Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI are not on that list. They are customers of it. A frontier lab choosing to train more slowly reduces its own compute consumption at the margin; it does not cause Amazon to cancel a substation.

## Training is the minority of the demand

The distinction that matters for the portfolio is between compute bought to **train** models and compute bought to **serve** them. A frontier slowdown hits the first. Only the first.

Training is lumpy, concentrated in a handful of labs, and a shrinking share of total compute as deployment scales. Inference is the opposite: diffuse, growing with usage rather than with ambition, and increasingly the thing hyperscalers build custom silicon to serve more cheaply. The industry's own framing is that training gets the headlines and inference takes the profits. Enterprise adoption broadening and inference workloads scaling into production are the reasons cloud backlogs are growing, and those backlogs are contractual — they do not care how fast GPT-6 arrives.

There is a second-order point worth stating. If frontier *capability* growth slows while *adoption* continues, inference demand may rise faster, not slower: a stable model generation is easier to build products on than one that obsoletes your integration every four months.

## The binding constraints are physical

The buildout is not currently constrained by how ambitious the labs are. It is constrained by power interconnection queues, transformers, turbines, land and skilled electrical labour. None of those loosens because three CEOs published essays. If anything, the schedule risk in this sector has always run the other way — projects slip because a substation is late, not because a model was delayed.

## What the market has already priced

This is the strongest argument against acting on the news: **a deceleration is substantially priced already.**

NVIDIA trades at **10.4× FY2029 consensus EPS** and 9.7× FY2030. That is not a bubble multiple; it is a multiple that assumes the cycle matures. Consensus itself has NVIDIA's EPS **peaking at $23.00 in FY2030 and declining to $20.00 in FY2031** even as revenue rises from $687B to $1.12T — the Street is already modelling margin compression and competitive erosion at the end of its own forecast. The dispersion is enormous (FY2031 EPS estimates run $13.46 to $23.65), which is the honest signal that nobody knows.

Meanwhile the infrastructure layer has already taken the hit the thesis calls for. **CRDO is 43% below its high, ALAB 39%, VRT 34%** — while hyperscaler capex guidance rose 80%. Those three have de-rated *into* accelerating spend. Selling them now is selling the correction rather than avoiding it.

## Where the real risk is

Cutting weight on this news would be acting on the wrong risk. The genuine risks in this book are:

1. **Concentration, not direction.** The growth sleeve holds NVDA 6.5%, MRVL 4.0%, TSM 4.0%, CRDO 4.0%, AMD 3.0%, NXPI 3.5%, KEYS 3.5% — roughly 28% in one correlated theme, plus VST at 6.0% which is the same trade in a different wrapper. If the AI capex cycle turns, these do not diversify each other.
2. **AMD specifically.** Up 59% above its 200-day and within 4% of its high, after a move from $154 to $560 in a year. That is a position where the valuation risk is position-specific and large.
3. **Custom silicon displacing merchant GPUs.** Every hyperscaler building its own accelerator is a structural headwind to NVDA and a tailwind to the design-services and interconnect layer.

## Recommendation

**Do not reduce AI weight on the frontier-slowdown news.** The pledge addresses capability governance; the spending is committed by parties who did not make it, for workloads that are not what the pledge restricts, against physical constraints the pledge does not touch, at multiples that already embed a slowdown.

**Do consider two things on other grounds:**

- **Trim AMD** into strength on valuation and position risk, not on this thesis. It is the position where price has run furthest ahead of the fundamental debate.
- **Address theme concentration** at the Q4 rebalance. ~28% of the growth sleeve in correlated AI infrastructure plus 6% in VST is a single factor bet whether or not the thesis is right. That is a sizing decision, and it should be made deliberately rather than as a reaction to an essay.

**What would change this view:** a hyperscaler cutting capex guidance on a quarterly call. That is the only evidence that would matter, and it would arrive from Amazon, Google, Microsoft or Meta — not from a frontier lab.
