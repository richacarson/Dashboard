# Belarus Potash and Nutrien: Quantifying the 21 September Headline

*Research note — 21 September 2026. Prepared in response to the President's Truth Social post that the US is "working on a massive Deal" to buy Belarusian potash at "substantially less" than it pays Canada, and NTR's 3.9% fall on the day. Pre-tax. Prices as of the 21 September session.*

---

## Summary

**The instinct is right — NTR is the most exposed name on the board — but the arithmetic says the market has already priced roughly the whole plausible effect, and it has priced it twice before.**

Nutrien's own CEO has already quantified the incremental supply at **approximately one million tonnes**, arriving slowly and at a structural cost disadvantage. That is **~1.4% of global shipments**. Today's 3.9% move took $1.45 billion of market value out, which at 9× EV/EBITDA prices a **$11 per tonne** decline in realised potash price — about 4.2% off the $266/t Nutrien realised in the first half.

Three facts materially undercut the headline:

1. **US sanctions on Belaruskali were already eased in March 2026**, following a December 2025 deal. This is not new legal access. It is the third time this trade has been made.
2. **Belarus is already exporting 12 million tonnes, above pre-sanction levels** — predominantly to China. The supply is already in the world market. A US purchase redirects trade flows; it does not create new tonnes.
3. **US potash is already cheaper than the world price.** NOLA is $335–345/t against a global benchmark of $395.50/t. The premise that America overpays Canada is not supported by the screens.

**Recommendation: hold NTR at 4.0%. Do not add on the dip, and do not cut on the headline.** The reason to be cautious on NTR is the potash cycle and thin earnings coverage, not Belarus.

---

## 1. What was actually announced

A post stating the United States "is working on a massive Deal with respect to the purchase of Potash from Belarus," with pricing "for substantially less than we are currently paying to Canada."

There is no signed agreement, no volume, no price, no counterparty and no timeline. Treating a statement of intent as a supply event is what produced the move.

## 2. What is actually new — very little

| Date | Event | NTR reaction |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 2025 | Trump deal: US to lift Belarus potash sanctions for 123 political prisoners | **−4%** |
| Dec 2025 | Bloomberg: *"Global potash market shrugs off US move to end Belarus sanctions"* | faded |
| Mar 2026 | US formally eases sanctions on Belaruskali and the Belarusian Potash Company after a 250-prisoner release | — |
| **Sep 2026** | **"Working on a massive Deal"** | **−3.9%** |

The legal barrier came down six months ago. Today's news is commercial intent layered on sanctions relief that already exists, and the last two times the market traded this theme the effect faded because the physical constraints did not move.

## 3. Belarus is already exporting — the tonnes are not new

Before sanctions, Belaruskali exported 10–11 Mt to 107 countries and held more than 20% of the global market. It is now on track for **more than 12 Mt — above pre-sanction levels** — with the bulk going to China.

This is the point most commentary misses. **Potash is fungible and globally traded.** If Belarusian tonnes sail to New Orleans instead of Qingdao, the Chinese tonnes have to come from somewhere, and Canadian tonnes displaced from the US Gulf go to Brazil or Asia. The world balance is unchanged. What changes is the freight map and, at the margin, netbacks.

**Nutrien CEO Ken Seitz has put the genuinely incremental figure at approximately one million tonnes**, coming back slowly, and noted Belarusian producers face hurdles that raise their costs. Against global shipments of roughly 72 Mt — implied by Nutrien's own guidance that its 14.2–14.8 Mt is "consistent with our historical share" — **1 Mt is about 1.4% of supply.**

**The logistics constraint is real and durable.** Belarus lost access to Klaipeda in Lithuania and depends on Russian ports. EU and UK sanctions remain in force. Rabobank's agriculture desk flags exactly this: the question marks are logistical. Saskatchewan to the US Midwest by rail is structurally cheaper than Belarus to the US Gulf by sea via a Russian port.

## 4. The pricing premise does not hold

| | $/tonne |
|---|---:|
| NOLA (US benchmark) | **$335–345** |
| Global benchmark | **$395.50** |
| Five-year average | $490 |
| NTR realised, H1 2026 (net, mine gate) | $266 |

The US is already buying below the world price. Canadian potash also enters the US **tariff-free under USMCA** and has been explicitly exempted from the tariff rounds, because tariffing it raises costs for American farmers — the constituency the post names.

## 5. Sizing the exposure

Potash is a third of the company, not all of it.

**Nutrien H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA, $3,535M:**

| Segment | $M | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retail | 1,239 | 35.1% |
| **Potash** | **1,236** | **35.0%** |
| Nitrogen | 1,117 | 31.6% |
| Phosphate | 80 | 2.3% |

**Potash unit economics, H1 2026:** 7,453 kt sold at $266/t net against $117/t cash cost — a $149/t margin. FY2026 guidance is 14.2–14.8 Mt with consolidated adjusted EBITDA around $5.0–5.5B.

**At 14.5 Mt, every $1/tonne of realised price is $14.5M of EBITDA.**

## 6. What today's move already prices

NTR fell from $77.09 to $74.065, taking market capitalisation from $37.01B to $35.55B — **$1.45 billion of value**.

| Assumed multiple | Implied potash price decline |
|---|---:|
| 8× EV/EBITDA | −$12.5/t (−4.7%) |
| 9× EV/EBITDA | **−$11.1/t (−4.2%)** |
| 10× EV/EBITDA | −$10.0/t (−3.8%) |

**So the market has capitalised a permanent ~$11/tonne hit to realised potash prices.** Set that against 1 Mt of incremental, higher-cost, logistically constrained supply representing 1.4% of a market where the US price is already below the world price.

That is a full, arguably generous, discount for the news. It is not an obvious overreaction to buy, and it is certainly not an underreaction to sell into.

## 7. Cross-checks from the tape

| | Today |
|---|---:|
| **NTR** | **−3.92%** |
| IPI (US potash) | −3.03% |
| MOS (potash + phosphate) | −2.70% |
| CF (nitrogen — no potash) | −1.90% |
| **ICL (Israeli potash)** | **−0.47%** |

Two things stand out. CF, which produces no potash, fell 1.9% — so a meaningful share of the complex move is sector sympathy rather than potash-specific repricing. And ICL, a genuine potash producer, barely moved, because its Dead Sea output serves Europe and Asia rather than the US Gulf. **The market is pricing a North American trade-flow event, not a global supply shock.** That is the correct read, and it is consistent with the fundamentals above.

## 8. Where the real risk is

Not Belarus. Two other things:

**A tariff on Canadian potash.** This is the opposite policy and the genuinely dangerous one. Washington imposed 50% tariffs on C$28B of Canadian goods after talks collapsed on 21 August; potash has so far been carved out under USMCA. If that exemption goes, NTR faces a direct netback hit on roughly 80% of US imports, and Nutrien has publicly warned it would land on American farmers. **Watch the exemption, not the Belarus headline.**

**The cycle, and the earnings coverage.** Potash at $395.50 is 19% below its five-year average of $490. Consensus has NTR's EPS *declining* from $4.93 (2027) to $4.42 (2030) — and 2029 and 2030 rest on a single analyst estimate. At $74.07 the stock trades at 15.0× 2027 and 16.7× 2030 on a falling earnings line. That is the more durable argument for not adding here, and it has nothing to do with Belarus.

## 9. Recommendation

- **Hold NTR at its 4.0% target weight.** The headline is largely already priced, twice-traded, and rests on supply that is mostly already in the market.
- **Do not add on the dip.** The discount is fair rather than excessive, and the forward earnings line is falling.
- **Do not cut on the headline.** Selling a 1.4% supply story after a 3.9% fall is selling the reaction.
- **Set a real trigger:** a removal of the USMCA potash exemption, or a signed Belarus contract with disclosed volume above 2 Mt/yr. Either would be a genuine change of facts. A post saying "working on" is not.

**What would change the view bullishly:** confirmation that Belarusian logistics remain capped below ~1 Mt into North America, plus NOLA holding above $335, would make today's discount look excessive.
