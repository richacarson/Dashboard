
# Oil Shock Delayed Effects — Q2 Earnings Disconnect & July Rebalance Playbook

## Thesis

We are **not yet feeling the full effects** of the oil shock that began with the Iran war on February 28, 2026. Brent crude closed at $112.10/bbl on May 18, 2026 — up 45%+ since hostilities began — with the Strait of Hormuz still largely closed (previously carrying ~20% of global crude and LNG). Academic research (Hamilton 1983, 2003; Kilian 2009) consistently shows that oil shocks transmit to corporate earnings with a **3-4 quarter lag**, meaning the February 2026 shock would begin hitting earnings in **Q4 2026 / Q1 2027**. Yet Wall Street consensus has Q2 2026 S&P 500 earnings growing +20.5% YoY, with estimates actually *rising* 2.1% in April — the largest first-month increase in 5 years. This is an unprecedented divergence. The July rebalance is our window to position before the lag catches up.

## The Historical Evidence: Oil Shocks Hit Earnings on a Delay

### Academic Consensus

The peak economic impact of an oil shock arrives **3-4 quarters (roughly 12 months)** after the price spike. Transmission is uneven — oil-sensitive sectors (transport, chemicals) feel it within 1-2 quarters, while oil-moderate sectors show earnings declines becoming pronounced around quarter 6, reaching peak losses by quarter 11.

Every post-WWII U.S. recession except 2020 was preceded by a significant oil price spike (Hamilton 2011, Federal Reserve 2014).

### Episode-by-Episode Lag Data

| Oil Shock | Price Move | Lag to Earnings Impact | Lag to Recession | Market Decline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1973 Arab Embargo** | $3 → $12 (4x) | 1-2 quarters | 1 month (Nov 1973) | -41% over 12 months; 6 years to recover |
| **1979 Iran Revolution** | $15 → $40 (2.7x) | 2-3 quarters | Fed rate hikes triggered 1980-82 double-dip | -6.2% initial, then Fed-driven recession |
| **1990 Gulf War** | $17 → $41 (2.4x) | 1-2 quarters (short shock) | Near-simultaneous (July 1990) | -20% peak-to-trough |
| **2008 Oil Spike** | $90 → $147 | Earnings already declining; oil accelerated collapse | Dec 2007 (predated peak) | S&P EPS fell ~90% peak to Q4 2008 trough |
| **2022 Russia/Ukraine** | $80 → $120+ | Manifested as inflation (CPI 9.1%) rather than earnings recession | Technical recession Q1-Q2 2022 | -25% (S&P); Energy +65.7% |

### The 2022 Analog — And Why 2026 May Be Worse

The 2022 shock is the cleanest modern comparison, but the U.S. was a net energy exporter by then, which blunted the impact. Key differences in 2026:

- **Duration:** 2022 oil above $100 lasted ~5 months. The current shock is approaching month 3 with no ceasefire in sight and Hormuz physically closed
- **Magnitude:** Hormuz closure removes ~20% of global crude transit vs. Russia sanctions that were partially circumvented
- **Fed position:** In 2022 the Fed was raising rates into the shock. In 2026 the Fed is at 3.50-3.75% and boxed in — can't cut (inflation) and shouldn't hike (slowing economy)
- **Consumer starting point:** Credit card delinquencies already at highest since 2012, consumer sentiment at 53.3

### Why the Disconnect Exists

1. **AI capex is real:** $200B+ committed to AI infrastructure is non-discretionary and insulated from oil
2. **Energy earnings surge masks weakness:** Energy sector's +45% earnings growth pulls up the headline number even as consumer-facing sectors deteriorate
3. **Lag hasn't hit yet:** The 3-4 quarter transmission delay means Q2 reports (April-June activity) largely predate the full impact
4. **Analyst anchoring:** Bottom-up estimates are notoriously slow to adjust to macro regime shifts — they didn't catch the 2008 collapse until Q3 either

### When the Shoe Drops

If the historical 3-4 quarter lag holds:

| Quarter | What Happens |

| **Q3 2026 (reporting Oct-Nov)** | First real impact. Consumer discretionary misses. Margin compression in industrials. Guidance cuts. |
| **Q4 2026 (reporting Jan-Feb 2027)** | Full transmission. Broad earnings declines outside energy/defense. Potential recession confirmation. |
| **Q1 2027** | Peak earnings impact if oil stays elevated. Recovery begins only if ceasefire or oil normalizes. |

## Downstream Effects Already Materializing

### What's Hitting Now (Q2 2026)

- **Jet fuel:** $230/bbl. American Airlines warned of $4B+ earnings reduction. United cutting 5% of routes. Fares up ~9% (IATA)
- **Gas prices:** U.S. average $3.99/gal; diesel $5.40/gal (highest in real terms in 2+ years)
- **Shipping:** USPS imposed first-ever fuel surcharge (8% on packages). Maersk added surcharges
- **Consumer pass-through:** Fuel price → consumer prices takes 1-2 months for fuel, 2-4 months for goods
- **China PPI:** Returned to growth for first time in 3 years on energy costs
- **IEA warning:** Global inventories depleting at record pace

### What's Coming (Q3-Q4 2026)

- Manufacturing input cost squeeze (chemicals, plastics, fertilizer-intensive industries)
- Consumer spending contraction as gas + goods inflation compounds
- Airline earnings collapse (unless fares can offset, which reduces demand)
- Housing slowdown if rates stay elevated (Fed can't cut into oil inflation)

## Sector Performance During Oil Shocks — Historical Data

### Annual Sector Returns in Oil Shock Years

| Sector | 2007 (pre-GFC spike) | 2008 (GFC) | 2022 (Russia/Ukraine) | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy** | +34.4% | -34.9% | **+65.7%** | Massive outperformance in supply shocks |
| **Consumer Staples** | +14.2% | -14.0% | -0.6% | Best defensive sector |
| **Healthcare** | +7.2% | -22.8% | -1.9% | Strong defense, limited oil input |
| **Utilities** | +19.4% | -29.0% | +1.6% | Defensive but rate-sensitive |
| **Consumer Discretionary** | -12.8% | -33.5% | **-37.0%** | Worst-hit sector consistently |
| **Technology** | +16.3% | -43.1% | -28.2% | High beta, sells off hard |
| **Financials** | -18.6% | -55.3% | -10.5% | Credit deterioration compounds |
| **Industrials** | +12.3% | -39.9% | -5.5% | Mixed — defense wins, transport loses |
| **Materials** | +22.2% | -45.7% | -12.3% | Input cost pressure vs. metals benefit |

**Key pattern:** In supply-driven oil shocks (1973, 1990, 2022 — our closest analog), **energy massively outperforms** while consumer discretionary and tech are the worst hit. Markets typically recover within 3-6 months unless the shock triggers a macro regime change (stagflation).

## Risks to This Analysis

1. **Ceasefire rally.** If Hormuz reopens, oil drops to $70-80, and we're caught underweight in high-beta names that rip. Energy overweight gives back gains quickly.
2. **AI earnings power overcomes oil drag.** If AI capex drives enough earnings growth to offset the oil shock, the 3-4 quarter lag may be muted. The 2022 analog (no official recession despite $120 oil) supports this.
3. **U.S. net exporter status.** The U.S. produces ~13M bbl/day. High oil prices boost domestic energy more than they drag the broader economy. The transmission may be weaker than historical precedents when the U.S. was a net importer.
4. **Consumer resilience surprises.** If labor market holds (unemployment 4.4% — elevated but not crisis), consumer spending may slow rather than collapse.
5. **Being too early.** Markets can stay irrational through Q2 earnings season if the headline numbers look strong. The pain trade is being defensive while the market rallies on lagging data.
