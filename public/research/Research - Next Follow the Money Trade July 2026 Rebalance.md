
# The Next Follow-the-Money Trade — July 2026 Rebalance Candidates

## Thesis

In April we bought CRDO and MRVL on the optical interconnect thesis — copper physically can't carry 400G signals beyond ~1 meter, NVIDIA was forced to commit $4B to optics suppliers, and the market was still focused on GPU scarcity. Both doubled. The question for late July: **where does the AI buildout physically break next, and who gets paid to fix it?**

The repeatable pattern has four ingredients:
1. **Hard physical constraint** — physics or manufacturing capacity, not sentiment
2. **Forced buyer with deep pockets** — someone who MUST spend to keep building
3. **Pure-play beneficiary with scarce capacity** — supply that takes years to add
4. **Market looking elsewhere** — the constraint isn't in consensus estimates yet

The macro frame makes the screen easy to run: hyperscaler 2026 capex is $630-725B, up 62-77% YoY. Satya Nadella has said publicly that Microsoft has **GPUs sitting in inventory it cannot plug in** for lack of powered shells. Of the ~16 GW announced US data center pipeline for 2026, only ~5 GW is under construction — 30-50% of planned projects are expected to slip, mostly waiting on electrical equipment. The bottleneck has officially moved from chips to electrons, steel, and cooling.

**The catch: the first-order power trade is crowded.** GEV is +76% YTD at ~50x forward. Vertiv +87% YTD at 46x. Bloom Energy +1,064% in a year at 27x sales. Powell +345%. Micron +226% at a $1T cap. Those were the 2025 trades. The 2026 opportunity is in the **second derivatives** — names where the same physical constraint flows through but the earnings inflection isn't in the stock yet.

### Key Data Points Behind the Map

**Transformers:** US lead times average 128 weeks; generator step-up units 144 weeks; transmission-class 3-6 years. Prices +77% since 2019. A transformer plant takes 3-5 years to build and certify — supply physically cannot catch up this decade.

**Gas turbines:** GE Vernova backlog hit 100 GW in Q1 2026 (83 GW at YE2025), targeting 110 GW. Effectively sold out through 2030, taking 2031+ orders with 10-20% price growth on new bidding.

**Gensets:** Large genset lead times stretched to 107 weeks at CAT/Cummins/Rolls-Royce. Cummins' large-genset backlog runs into early 2027.

**Liquid cooling:** Penetration went from ~3% of data centers in 2021 to ~37% in 2026. Average rack density 27 kW (+69% YoY); AI racks at 60-100kW+ require direct-to-chip. Market: $6B (2026) → $27B (2035E).

**HBM:** SK Hynix and Micron entire 2026 output sold out. HBM3E prices +20%. But MU +226% YTD at $1T cap — the constraint is fully discovered.

**ABF substrates:** Supply shortfall projected at 10% in 2H26, 21% in 2027, **42% in 2028**. Ajinomoto holds 80-90% share (near 100% in high-end film). ~50% of consumption is now AI chips.

**Eaton's estimate:** 228 GW total US data center demand backlog ≈ 12 years of demand at 2025 build rates.

## The Buy List — Ranked by CRDO-Likeness

### 1. Generac (GNRC) — The Un-Priced Hyperscaler Approval — TOP PICK

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Market Cap** | ~$15.4B |
| **52-Week** | +104% |
| **Forward P/E** | 28.5x |
| **3-Yr EPS Growth Est.** | ~30% |
| **DC Backlog** | $400M — built from colocators BEFORE hyperscaler vendor approvals |

**The setup:** Generac is in final-stage vendor approval with multiple hyperscalers. The market still models it as a home-standby generator company (hurricane stock). Its data center business targets 10-15% of a $15B/year large-megawatt genset market where lead times are 107 weeks and incumbents (CAT, Cummins) are sold out into 2027. Hunterbrook's investigative work found Stargate construction permits specify an engine configuration "almost no one sells, except Generac."

**Why it's CRDO-like:** The catalyst (hyperscaler approvals) is identifiable, dated (2026), and NOT in consensus estimates. When approvals land, the orders are step-function, not gradual. Both research streams independently ranked this the single most asymmetric setup in the map.

**Risk:** +104% in a year already; if approvals slip or fail, it's a $15B company at 28x with a decelerating residential business. Position size accordingly.

**Sleeve:** Growth Hybrid.

### 2. EMCOR Group (EME) — The Valuation Anomaly of the Physical Buildout

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Market Cap** | ~$36.8B |
| **Forward P/E** | **27.9x** — vs. Comfort Systems (FIX) 41.5x, Sterling (STRL) 44x |
| **Q1 2026 Revenue** | $4.63B, +19.7% — record |
| **Business** | Electrical & mechanical construction — the people who actually wire the data centers |

**The setup:** Every data center needs electrical contractors, and the three big publics (FIX, EME, STRL) all have record backlogs. FIX is up ~295% in a year and trades at 41.5x. STRL backlog +131% at 44x. EME has the same end-market dynamics, record results — and trades at a near-market multiple. It's the cheapest way to own the physical buildout, with the most margin of safety.

**Why it's CRDO-like:** Not a moonshot — it's the "boring compounder" expression. The market re-rated its two peers and somehow left EME behind. Quality + growth + relative value rarely line up this cleanly.

**Risk:** Construction is cyclical; if data center starts slow (30-50% of 2026 projects slipping), backlog conversion slows. Mitigant: slips are due to equipment shortages, not demand — the work still happens, later.

**Sleeve:** Growth Hybrid (yield too small for Dividend sleeve).

### 4. Hubbell (HUBB) — Grid Scarcity at a Non-Scarcity Multiple

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|

**Risk:** Slower growth (~9% EPS) than the high-fliers; utility T&D spending is steadier but less explosive than hyperscale direct.

## Risks to the Whole Thesis

1. **AI capex pause.** If hyperscaler capex growth flattens in 2027 (Microsoft/Meta digestion phase), every name on the list de-rates. Mitigant: the buys are the cheapest expressions, with the most non-AI revenue ballast (HUBB utility T&D, KMI gas, EME commercial construction, GNRC residential).

2. **The bear market scenario.** Our April assessment put a normal bear at 40-50% probability in 12 months. Industrials and cyclicals fall hard in either bear case. These are long-term positions — be prepared to hold through a 25-35% drawdown or size entries in tranches.

3. **Project slippage cascade.** 30-50% of 2026 data center projects are already slipping. If slips become cancellations (AI ROI disappointment), backlogs deflate. Watch: hyperscaler capex guidance revisions, not announcements.

4. **GNRC approval failure.** The top pick's catalyst is binary. If hyperscaler vendor approvals don't land by Q4 2026, the DC thesis stalls and we own a home-standby company at 28x.

5. **Power constraint resolution.** Faster-than-expected grid buildout, SMR acceleration, or efficiency breakthroughs (better chips per watt) would deflate the scarcity premium across the complex.

6. **Crowding migration.** The market found GEV/VRT/BE in 2025. It may find GNRC/EME/HUBB by Q3 2026 — which is fine if we're in first, but entries matter. Don't chase any of these up 20%+ from current levels before July.
